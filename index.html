fatto.

A ledger of the meals and the moments you share with people. Photographs are made with the camera inside the app, at the table, and kept here. One photograph is one moment, and each moment is numbered in the order it was kept.

Why did my photographs turn black and white?

Because they are meant to. A photograph holds its color for three days from the moment it was taken, then turns a warm black and white and stays that way. Nothing is lost when this happens. The original is untouched on the device, and the black and white is worked out fresh each time the photograph is shown.

Do they ever come back in color?

Yes, once a year. On a moment's own date it returns to color for that whole day, then settles back. A moment kept on 29 February returns on 28 February in years without one.

Why can I not add photographs from my library?

The camera in the app is the only way in. That is what keeps the ledger a record of evenings that happened rather than a collection assembled later. There is no import, and there are no filters or edits beyond the two treatments the app applies itself.

Why can I no longer edit the labels?

Who, What, and Remember can be changed while a moment is still in color. When it turns, they lock with it.

Where are my photographs, and are they backed up?

On your device. Back up to iCloud is on by default and copies them into the private database of your own iCloud account, so a new phone restores the ledger with its numbering, labels, and fade states intact. Settings shows a plain status line, including when a backup is waiting because iCloud is full or the phone is offline.

How do I get a photograph out?

Open the moment and choose Save to Photos. It saves as it looks at that moment, in its current treatment. The same actions sit behind a long press on the photograph in the ledger. There is also a switch in Settings that keeps every original in Photos in full color as you shoot, which is off unless you turn it on.

Can I delete something?

Yes, behind a plain confirmation. A deleted moment waits in Recently deleted, in Settings, for 30 days, and Restore brings it back. After 30 days it is gone.

Delete a moment within 72 hours of keeping it and the numbers close up behind it, because that window is for correcting a mis-tap. Delete one after that and its number stays empty, because by then the number is part of the record.

The subscription

fatto. works without one. The subscription is reached by the word "subscribe" at the top of the ledger, and it is the only place the app asks. It comes as an annual or a monthly plan, with the price shown on that screen and in the App Store. Anyone who has not had it before starts with one free week; Apple allows that once per Apple Account, and the screen says which of the two applies to you before you buy.

It carries: color for a week instead of three days, 280 characters on Remember instead of 140, reminders to fill a moment in the next morning, private moments, alternate app icons, and sharing without the small fatto. watermark.

Restore purchases sits on the same screen, for a new phone or a reinstall. Cancelling is done where every subscription is, in the App Store through your Apple Account in iOS Settings.

If it lapses, nothing is deleted and nothing is truncated. A moment that was keeping its color for a week finishes that week. Longer notes stay exactly as they were written. A private moment stays private, and can still be revealed and unmarked. Only new moments go back to three days.

The lock, and private moments

The lock is free and off unless you turn it on. With it on, the ledger asks for Face ID when the app is opened cold, and frosts over when the app goes to the background, so it is frosted in the app switcher too. The device passcode always works as well. A phone with no passcode set cannot lock, and Settings says so plainly.

A private moment is blurred where it sits in the ledger. Reveal asks for Face ID and shows it for as long as you are looking. Marking is manual and never automatic, and a private moment is backed up like any other.

Filling a moment in later

When the three boxes are still open, Remind me later asks iPhone for permission at that tap and nothing sooner. The reminder arrives at nine the next morning and opens that moment. Declining the permission breaks nothing: the boxes stay open and you can fill them whenever you like.
